# Using the dashboard in class

A 40-minute introduction period, plus what to do in the weeks after.

---

## Curriculum links

| Area | What the app supports |
|---|---|
| TWAU / EVS | Food groups, where food comes from, plants and animals as food sources, cleanliness and water, growing and changing |
| Mathematics | Data handling — collecting your own data, reading bar graphs, comparing quantities, tallies, measurement in centimetres, subtraction of two measurements |
| Computational thinking | Classification and pattern recognition — deciding which group a food belongs to is exactly the sorting skill the CT strand asks for |
| Health and wellbeing | Routine, hydration, sleep, outdoor play |

---

## Period 1 — Introduction (40 minutes)

**Before class:** open the app on the smartboard, go to *Teacher corner*, set a PIN and
type the school name.

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Ask: *"What did you eat this morning?"* Collect five answers on the board. |
| 5–12 min | Show the empty thali on the smartboard. Explain the five parts. Add the five answers as a class and watch the plate fill. Ask which part is still empty. |
| 12–18 min | Introduce Everyday foods and Sometimes foods. Say clearly: **no food is a bad food.** Sometimes foods belong to festivals and birthdays. Show the little side bowl. |
| 18–30 min | Children come up in pairs and make their own card. Ask them to use first name and roll number only. |
| 30–36 min | Each child logs today's food and water on the smartboard, or on the class tablet/computer in turns. |
| 36–40 min | Set the routine: *every day after lunch, two minutes, fill your card.* |

**Do not** turn this into a competition between children, and do not read one child's plate
aloud in a way that compares it with another's.

---

## Period 2 — Reading our own graphs (Maths, after one week)

1. Open *My week* for a volunteer.
2. Ask the four questions on the screen. Let children answer aloud.
3. Everyone copies the food-group bar graph into their Maths notebook, with a scale of
   1 square = 1 day.
4. Ask them to write two sentences: *"The tallest bar is ___"* and *"Next week I will
   try to raise ___"*.
5. Extension: make a tally chart of the whole class's favourite fruit, then draw it as a
   bar graph.

This is real data handling practice, because the data is theirs.

---

## Period 3 — Where does food come from? (TWAU)

1. Open the *Learn* tab. Today's foods are already sorted into plants and animals.
2. Ask: *which column is longer today?*
3. Take three foods and trace them backwards — roti to wheat to a field; milk to a cow to
   grass; egg to a hen.
4. Group work: each group picks one food and draws its journey from farm to plate.

---

## Monthly — the measuring day

Fix one day a month, say the first Monday.

1. Revise the method: no shoes, back to the wall, heels together, eyes straight ahead, flat
   scale on the head, mark the wall, measure with a tape.
2. Children measure in pairs and enter their height in the *I grow* tab.
3. From the second month onwards each child can subtract to find how much they grew.
   That is a real subtraction problem with a real answer.

Keep the tone on *measuring accurately*, which is a science skill. Do not comment on any
child being taller or shorter than another, and do not display heights side by side.

## Language to use, and language to avoid

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| "That is a Sometimes food." | "That is junk food." |
| "Your plate has four colours today." | "Your plate is bad today." |
| "What could you add tomorrow?" | "You should not eat that." |
| "You measured carefully. Well done." | "You are shorter than last year's average." |
| "Everyone's body grows at its own speed." | any comparison between two children |

---

## A note on children who log very little

Some children eat what is available at home, and that may be one or two foods. The app must
never make that child feel behind. If a child's plate is often one or two parts, treat it as
information for you as a teacher — a quiet word, a note to the mid-day meal in-charge, or a
conversation with the family — not as something to raise in front of the class.

If you notice a child skipping meals, becoming anxious about food, or using the app to eat
less, stop their use of the app and speak to the parents and the school counsellor. A habit
tracker is a good tool for most children and the wrong tool for a few.

---

## Weekly two-minute routine that makes it stick

- **Monday:** show one anonymous class total on the board — "last week our class drank
  water 6+ glasses on 84 days altogether."
- **Wednesday:** one child explains their favourite badge and how they earned it.
- **Friday:** teacher downloads the CSV backup.
